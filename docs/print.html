Story Studio — A Writer’s Companion

Welcome to Story Studio

Edition: English · বাংলা

A story can begin with a promise, an image, or a question you cannot leave alone. Story Studio is an exploration of a workspace that helps you develop that beginning while keeping the story yours.

This public companion describes the writer experience we want to deliver and the parts we can already demonstrate. Studio itself is a private, local, experimental application. Publishing this book does not make the application public, downloadable, or supported. There is no public Studio release announced here.

You decide what matters, what becomes true, and what stays unresolved. The intended collaboration is straightforward: you imagine; Studio helps organize; an LLM may elaborate; you decide what to keep. A welcome surprise can enrich your story without taking it over.

The first three chapters form this initial book foundation. The workspace orients you, and the tiny-story exercise demonstrates a complete structural beginning-to-ending journey using real controls. The larger-story working method and Maya mystery workshop now extend that foundation. The other later chapters remain brief outlines.

The exercise and screenshots were verified on 8 November 2026 in the private build. Its interface currently says Storybuilder · Narrative Workshop; this book uses Story Studio for the broader vision. Screenshots are real application captures using a fictional sample, not interface mockups.

You do not need the private application to read this book. To perform an Available exercise, you need access to a private local build provided separately. This book does not provide that access.

The Writer’s Workspace

Edition: English · বাংলা

Available — orientation to the verified private workspace.

Think of the workspace as a place to keep the story behind the words: who wants something, what happens, and what follows. The graph is one way to see those connections; it is not the finished prose.

The real private workspace after the first exercise. Open the full-size image to inspect small labels.

At the top, Your stories lets you return to saved work. Interface language switches English/Bangla without translating story content. Story Properties, beside the story title, lets you rename the story and edit its subtitle, synopsis, genre, story language and author notes; these changes save with the project. Start a new story creates another project. A new story begins empty, so you can introduce the people and circumstances you actually want.

You can enter text in any Unicode-supported language, regardless of the interface or declared story language. No automatic translation occurs.

Story journeys keeps your place. Beside it, the intention buttons ask what you want to do next: expand, deepen, escalate, reveal, connect, redirect, resolve, or conclude. Below them, the development panel offers ways to express that intention. The panel can ask for a missing essential detail before showing a preview.

The preview is a pause for your judgment. Read the proposed meaning, correct a binding if needed, and confirm only when it expresses your intention. The graph changes after confirmation. Undo last decision can recover the most recent accepted decision; it is not an unrestricted edit of any earlier moment.

The status area shows the current situation and unresolved commitments. Resolving a goal and approving an ending are separate author choices. You may also deliberately approve an open ending; this first exercise uses a resolved one.

Envisioned — the broader workspace. A derived timeline, an integrated scaffold-to-prose action, a prose editor, and a review space for generated inventions belong to the intended experience. They are not present as integrated controls in this verified build. The later outlines describe them as possibilities, not instructions to find nonexistent buttons.

Local saving is available for the structured story. Closing a browser is different from deleting its saved project. The current application does not save a final prose manuscript as part of that workflow.

Your First Tiny Story

Edition: English · বাংলা

Available — complete exercise verified in the private application on 8 November 2026.

You need access to the current private build, open in your browser. This exercise ends with a small structured story, not an automatically generated prose manuscript.

A promise and a tear

Mina has promised to return her neighbor’s borrowed umbrella before the neighbor leaves. On the way, she notices a tear in it. She stitches the tear and returns the umbrella in time. Her promise is kept.

Your aim is to give Mina a goal, introduce that complication, connect the repair to it, and approve the ending. You do not need to supply a whole cast, a detailed setting, or exact dates.

1. Give the story a home

If the controls are in Bangla, choose English from অ্যাপের ভাষা at the top; in English this control is labeled Interface language. If they are already in English, leave the setting as it is.

Open Start a new story. Enter Umbrella draft as the Story title. Leave Random seed at its default and select Create story ↗.

Name the story; the seed does not need a creative decision for this lesson.

An empty workspace opens. Before adding events, try changing the story’s information.

Choose Story Properties beside the story title. Change Story title to The Borrowed Umbrella. You can also add a subtitle, synopsis, genre, and author notes. In this sample, the subtitle is A promise kept. These fields are optional. New stories currently start with English as Story language, regardless of the interface language. You can override it independently. Keep English for this exercise and select Save properties.

Renaming updates the title and Your stories without making a new story. Optional text, including mixed English/Bangla notes, is stored as written. Reopen Story Properties or reload to check it.

At the top of the application, Interface language changes the controls between English and বাংলা. Try Bangla, then return to English for the remaining English instructions. This choice is remembered in your browser. It does not change Story language, translate your writing, or create another story. Even unsaved property text remains while you switch.

The interface language is separate from the story’s language. For the full Bangla walkthrough, open the Bangla edition from the chapter’s language link.

Choose Expand among the intention buttons.

2. Give Mina something to want

Choose Establish a goal or motivation in the development panel.

For Whose goal is this?, select Introduce one instead…. Enter Mina under What is its name?, then choose Continue to preview.

The next question asks What do they want to achieve? Enter:

Return the borrowed umbrella before her neighbor leaves.

Choose Continue to preview again. Despite its name, that button can lead to another essential question before the preview appears.

Read the proposed meaning before confirming it.

Check that it introduces Mina and gives her the intended goal. Choose Confirm this development. Mina and her goal now appear in the graph, and the status lists an unresolved goal.

3. Let something complicate it

Keep Expand selected. Choose Another specific occurrence. Under What happened?, enter:

Mina notices a tear in the borrowed umbrella.

Choose Continue to preview, read the event, and select Confirm this development. The graph now includes the discovery. The goal is still unresolved: noticing the tear has not fulfilled the promise.

“Another specific occurrence” is the current event button’s label. You do not need to introduce a second character or a separately named umbrella object for this small lesson.

4. Bring the promise to a resolution

Choose Resolve, then Resolve a commitment through a specific development.

The current sample has one unresolved goal, so the preview can select it. Under What happens to resolve it?, enter:

Mina stitches the tear and returns the umbrella before her neighbor leaves.

Choose Continue to preview. Check that the target is Mina’s original goal.

Open Add detail or revise a binding, then choose Optional: Which event enables the resolution? Select Mina notices a tear in the borrowed umbrella. and choose Continue to preview. You may collapse the detail menu to read the preview.

The repair resolves the promise and is connected to the discovery that prompted it. “Cause” here records that narrative connection; it does not supply an exact time.

Select Confirm this development. The unresolved-goal count should now be zero. The app has recorded your intended resolution; it has not assessed the quality of the story’s prose or independently checked a clock deadline.

5. Decide that this story is finished

Choose Conclude. The panel switches to the existing ending choices. Select Approve a conclusion with every commitment resolved.

Your completion check: AUTHOR-APPROVED CONCLUSION, Ending: resolved, and Unresolved commitments · 0.

The status now confirms the approved ending without asking you to approve it again.

You did it: Mina has a desire, encounters a complication, acts because of it, and reaches an author-approved ending. The structured story saves locally. Select it again under Your stories to resume.

If something goes wrong

A preview says the wrong thing: before confirming, open Add detail or revise a binding, choose the relevant Revise: question, answer it, and use Continue to preview again. Read the corrected preview before confirming. This correction path was tested with the goal wording.

You approved an ending too soon: choose ↶ Undo last decision. It reopens the last approved ending while retaining its history. You can inspect the story and approve the conclusion again. This recovery was also tested. Undo affects the latest accepted decision; it is not a general editor for any earlier decision.

Make one small change

Optional variation: start a new version in which Mina finds the tear before setting out. Change the discovery wording and consider whether the same repair still follows. This variation is a creative invitation, not another verified step-by-step tutorial. You decide what changes the story’s meaning.

Continue with the larger-story working method and the Maya mystery workshop when you are ready to hold several goals and a deliberate loose end together.

“A Richer Story”

Edition: English · বাংলা

Available — working method verified with the Maya mystery on 8 November 2026.

A larger story asks you to hold several intentions at once. Our next exercise, The Letter That Arrived Three Years Late, gives Maya a practical danger to investigate, Nila a reason to resist her, and a question we deliberately leave unanswered. We construct these commitments rather than ask the app to invent a plot for us.

Keep three kinds of notes apart

What you meanWhere to put it in this exercise
Something established in the story worldA concrete fact with objective scope, or an explicitly authored event
Something a character suspectsA proposition with belief scope and its holder; it may be false
An alternative you have not chosenAuthor notes, or a draft you revise/cancel before confirmation
A question the ending should acknowledgeAn explicit goal left unresolved; a note alone is not a listed commitment

The story’s construction order is our working order. It is not a scene list, reader chronology or automatic prose outline. Even reader scope records a distinct assertion; it is not a complete revelation scheduler.

The repeatable control pattern

  1. Choose an intention, such as Expand, Reveal, Escalate, Connect, Redirect, or Resolve. These select available mechanisms; they do not evaluate the dramatic effect of your writing.
  2. Choose the specified mechanism. Answer the immediate question and select Continue to preview. Repeat until Proposed meaning appears; that button can lead to another question first.
  3. For a listed field not asked directly, open Add detail or revise a binding. Choose Optional: followed by its prompt, or Revise: if it already has an inferred/supplied answer. Enter or select its value, then Continue to preview. Repeat for the remaining fields. Fields inferred correctly need no resubmission.
  4. For a single existing entity, select its displayed name. For several participants or related commitments, select all named entries in the multi-select (use Cmd on macOS or Ctrl on Windows/Linux for separate entries). For a proposition whose value is a person, choose Choose an existing entity instead and select that person. If already in entity mode, select directly. Use Use a literal value instead for plain text.
  5. Read the whole preview, including actor, cause and goal bindings. Inferences are proposals; an incidental character name can bind the wrong actor. Revise it before Confirm this development. Text naming someone does not guarantee the intended structural relationship.
  6. Confirm only when meaning matches your decision. The graph changes at confirmation. To abandon an idea, use Cancel this development. To change a preview, revise it; do not confirm and hope to edit it later.

The detailed exercise gives every value needed. A reference to an earlier step means select that existing element, preserving identity. Selectors may show a type beside a label; this matters when Fact, Proposition and Belief labels look alike. The inspector shows truth and active for belief records; false is not the same as a missing belief.

Watch the cost of expressing an idea

The verified workshop contains 19 semantic developments plus a separate ending approval. For those 19, the app recorded 158 author responses in English and 156 in Bangla, with a median of 7 per development in either language. Those counters include intention/mechanism choices, supplied answers, optional-field selection, corrections and confirmation. They are not a count of questions alone or an estimate of writing time.

The preview revision exercise deliberately adds work. The meaningful decision “share a redacted copy” took 13 recorded responses and 20 instrumented control actions, including the rejected draft. The final resolution needed 11 recorded responses in English and 9 in Bangla; the English wording initially inferred the wrong actor, which was corrected before confirmation. Semantic accuracy was preserved, at a visible interaction cost.

Instrumented control actions count one text entry or multi-select operation as one action, not each keystroke or physical click. Recovery, the rejected cycle experiment, metadata setup and inspection are reported separately from the main construction. These are two automated rehearsals of one authored story, not a study of novice writers.

Watch the network grow

After the first six developments there are 7 nodes; after the conflict there are 17; the approved ending has 24 nodes and 49 connections. Some are support records for facts and belief changes, not additional plot beats. Do not equate node count with story richness.

In the final whole-network view, long labels become too small to read comfortably. Fit remains useful for orientation; the inspector and explicit connections are useful for reading. Arrange can move elements between layouts, so screen position is not a durable story reference. This is observed friction in a modest sample, not proof that hierarchy is the only solution. Existing focused subjourneys are a separate capability; this exercise stays in one workspace to observe the current flat network.

Continue directly to The Impossible Letter for the complete construction. The intervening time chapter remains a conceptual outline; it is not a prerequisite or an available timeline control.

“The Impossible Letter”

Edition: English · বাংলা

The Letter That Arrived Three Years Late

Three years after Arif’s death, his wife Maya receives a sealed letter in handwriting resembling his. It warns that the lower ward will flood at the next floodgate pressure test. Courier Nila knows more than she admits. Her brother supplied confidential maintenance records, and publishing the original letter could identify him.

This is an author’s workshop, with spoilers. We will choose a grounded explanation, not ask Studio to solve the mystery. Nila sent an old, genuine warning when a new test was announced. Maya must find evidence she can use without exposing the informant. An unexplained blue star on the envelope will remain open.

Complete the tiny story first, then read the larger-story working method. The result here is a structured narrative network, not prose, a sequence of scenes, or a reader-facing revelation schedule. Everything below is built with current controls; no hierarchical regrouping is required.

Follow a distant connection

After approval, use Or select a story element to select Maya’s redaction decision from step 18. In the inspector, follow the connection to the carbon-copy discovery from step 15; the CAUSES connection leads back to the discovery that made the decision possible. The inspector lists relation names and neighbors without labeling incoming/outgoing direction; use the graph arrows or the explicit bindings from steps 15 and 18 to verify direction. The stamp discovery and arrival are other branches; not every earlier event caused every later one.

The inspector also retains original_text, including the rejected first draft from step 18. That is provenance, not another event. The confirmed label and action contain the redacted-copy decision.

The element picker disappears once an element is selected. You can follow connection buttons or click graph nodes; reload to reset selection and bring back the picker. Fit shows the whole network; zoom in or use the inspector for full wording. Arrange changes the layout, not the story. Returning to a saved story preserves the graph and approved ending.

Try an author’s alternative

Try a separate story so you do not need to rewrite older accepted decisions. If you make the blue-star sender known, resolve that goal explicitly before asking for a fully closed ending. You decide whether the explanation is satisfying; Studio records the commitments you approve.
